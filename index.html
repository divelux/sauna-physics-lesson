Why Is the Top Bench Hotter?

A middle school lesson on warm air, density and convection. Students explain a real puzzle from the sauna with a safe colored-water model, their own classroom data and a particle drawing. No flame, no boiling water.

The puzzle

Sauna heater maker HUUM writes: Heat rises upward: the top bench is the hottest, while the lower bench feels gentler. It gives typical bench heights of 30, 60 and 105 cm (about 12, 24 and 41 in). It is one room with the same air. Why would the air by the top bench be hotter? Students answer that by the end of the class.

The lesson in six steps

  1. Engage, 5 min. Show the diagram and the quote. Students write a first guess.
  2. Explore A, 15 min. In a still container of room-temperature water, groups squeeze warm red water out at the bottom and ice-cold blue water at the top, and draw what happens: warm rises, cold sinks.
  3. Explore B, 10 min. Read three thermometers placed at the start of class on the floor, at desk height and up high, then plot temperature against height. A small or no difference is a result to discuss, not a failure.
  4. Explain, 10 min. A particle model: warmer particles move faster and spread out, so the fluid is less dense and rises. That movement is convection. Back to the sauna and its convection loop.
  5. Elaborate, 5 min. Rank the three benches and justify the ranking with the model.
  6. Evaluate, 5 min. Exit ticket: rewrite the first guess using particles, density and convection.

Safety

  • No heat sources. No kettles, hot plates, candles, lighters, heat lamps or light bulbs. The warmest thing in the lesson is warm water from the tap.
  • Warm, not hot. The teacher fills the warm cups and checks the water is comfortable to hold a finger in.
  • No climbing. Only the teacher places the high thermometer, from the floor.
  • Spills and stains. Wipe up water at once; use trays for food coloring. Digital or alcohol thermometers only, never mercury.

Core ideas used: PS3.A, the temperature of a system is proportional to the average internal kinetic energy and potential energy per atom or molecule; PS3.B, "Energy is spontaneously transferred out of hotter regions or objects and into colder ones."
